A message from our Chairperson

I’ve competed in YNEPF for over a decade now, and I’ve volunteered for just as long: as a spotter and loader, a referee, and for the last four years on the committee and exec, most recently as Chairperson. I’m writing to say I’ll be stepping down.

Letter

Before I ever competed, Kelly Shortland was the first person to rope me in, as a spotter and loader. I never really left. Thank you, Kelly, for that.

I want to be upfront about why I’m going, and then say the important part: thank you.

YNEPF is nearly a century old, founded in 1928, and it’s grown into a division of well over a hundred members running a full calendar of competitions every year. In 2022 I joined the strategy and graphics side, trying to make our lifting orders look sharper and give the November Open a bit more buzz. From there I moved into a more strategic role: testing new competitions, introducing a qualifying total, and building the franchise model that’s let gyms across the division host and run their own events, giving us some brilliant venues in the process. We moved payments away from PayPal to Square, making entry smoother for everyone, and introduced a volunteer points system, so that giving your time is properly recognised with entry discounts and other perks. And together, we’ve raised the bar on equipment, technology and presentation to the point where YNEPF is genuinely regarded as one of the better federations in the country. It’s been a privilege to help shape a small part of that story.

This sport has given me a huge amount back: a life, friends, a gym community I really care about, and people I know I’ll be close with for a long time to come.

Thank you

This year we grew the team behind the scenes further, and I want to use this to say a proper thank you to everyone currently giving their time on the exec and the committee.

A special thanks too to Becky Martin, who’s been part of YNEPF longer than I’ve been on the exec or committee, supporting in more ways than I could list, official or not, wherever she’s needed. If you’ve ever worn a YNE t-shirt, there’s a good chance she had something to do with it.

And to every referee, spotter, loader, table worker and runner who turns up week after week: this sport exists because of you.

That’s also, honestly, part of why I’m stepping away. Powerlifting takes and takes, and the number of people doing the giving is small against the number who turn up to compete. My own career outside the sport has grown considerably this year, and it’s taking more of my time and energy than I have left to give. I’ve also been managing a health condition, which has affected my training, how often I can compete, and if I’m honest, some of my love for the sport too. Between the two, I’ve become more distant from the division than I’d like, and that’s not fair on YNEPF or on me. I need a bit of my life back.

What happens next

I’m not disappearing overnight, and I’m not disappearing altogether. I plan to stay on through YNE Worlds 2026 and hand over cleanly at the AGM, with an EGM to follow later in the year if a new Chairperson isn’t confirmed there. After that, I’ll still be around as a referee and supporting where I can, just not in an official capacity. I leave knowing the team I’ve helped build is one of the strongest we’ve had: people who love this sport as much as I do and want to keep pushing it forward.

If I could have kept going, I’d have loved to finish the work on our website and entry systems, and to build on our relationship with adjacent divisions like the North West, running interdivisional competitions together, perhaps even bringing back the Roses Cup. Longer term, I’d love to see a national competition hosted in our division, using one of the fantastic facilities we have across our cities. I hope whoever takes this on can keep chasing that.

If you’ve ever thought about giving an hour or two back to the sport, now is the time. This division only exists because people choose to show up for it, and if you haven’t been involved yet, please do.

Ant White

Chairperson
